Using EDWIN

What you'll need

  • EDWIN installed and set up (see Getting Started (macOS) or Getting Started (Windows))

How long this takes

5 minutes to read, a lifetime to master.

How to talk to EDWIN

EDWIN works like a human assistant: just tell it what you need in plain language. You don't need special commands or syntax — though there are shortcuts if you want them.

Starting a conversation

Just type what you want EDWIN to do:

You: Brief me on this article

EDWIN: (Uses the briefing skill to create a summary)

You: I need help planning a project

EDWIN: (Uses the project-planner skill to break it down)

You: Switch to Work

EDWIN: Switched to Work.

EDWIN figures out which skill to use based on what you say. You can also ask EDWIN directly:

You: What can you help me with?

EDWIN: (Lists skills available in your current context)

What skills are

A skill is a focused way of working — a methodology for a specific kind of task. EDWIN comes with 23 built-in skills:

Skill What it does
analyst Rigorous data analysis with pattern detection
blog-writer Research-backed blog writing and revision
briefing Distills anything into an executive summary
executive-coach Career coaching for engineering leaders
intellectual-sparing-partner Pressure-tests your ideas through debate
project-planner Breaks projects into deliverables and tasks
prompter Optimizes prompts for AI tools
researcher Deep research with source evaluation
strategist Structured brainstorming from ideas to decisions
tutor Teaches concepts through questions, not answers
x-ghostwriter Writes X (Twitter) posts through interviews

...and several more for managing EDWIN itself (memory, contexts, creating skills, scheduling tasks).

Using a skill

You don't need to say the skill's name. Just say what you need, and EDWIN will route to the right skill:

You: Teach me how recursion works

EDWIN: (Uses the tutor skill, asks Socratic questions)

You: Help me plan my quarterly roadmap

EDWIN: (Uses the project-planner skill)

If you want to be explicit, you can name the skill:

You: Use the analyst skill on this dataset

EDWIN: (Applies that skill)

Understanding contexts

EDWIN organizes skills into three contexts: Global, Work, and Home. Think of them like modes you switch between.

The three contexts

  • Global: No filtering. All skills are equally relevant. Use this when you're working on mixed tasks or haven't decided on a focus.
  • Work: Professional tasks. Skills like analyst, briefing, project-planner, and executive-coach surface first. Use this during work hours.
  • Home: Personal projects and learning. Use this for side projects, studying, or household matters.

Important: Contexts don't restrict access. Every skill stays available in every context. Contexts just help EDWIN prioritize suggestions when you say something that could match multiple skills.

Switching contexts

Change your active context

You: Switch to Work

EDWIN: Switched to Work.

You: Switch to Home

EDWIN: Switched to Home.

You: Switch to Global

EDWIN: Switched to Global.

Your active context persists between conversations. EDWIN remembers where you left off.

Seeing what's available

List skills by context

You: List skills

EDWIN: (Shows all skills, grouped by context. Your active context appears first.)

You: What skills are in this context?

EDWIN: (Shows skills tagged for your current context, plus Global skills)

You: What context am I in?

EDWIN: You're in Work.

EDWIN's personality

EDWIN is modeled after Edwin Jarvis from the Iron Man stories — professional, composed, concise. It won't pad responses with filler or unnecessary explanation. When EDWIN says "three issues found — want the breakdown?" it means you can say yes to see details, or no to move on.

If you prefer more verbose responses, you can adjust that preference:

You: I'd like more detailed responses

EDWIN: (Adjusts your verbosity preference to "detailed")

To revert:

You: Go back to concise responses

EDWIN: (Sets verbosity back to "concise")

Asking EDWIN for help

EDWIN knows how EDWIN works. If you forget how to do something, just ask:

You: How do I create a new skill?

EDWIN: (Explains the workflow analyzer and skill creator)

You: How do I remember things between sessions?

EDWIN: (Explains the memory system)

You: Show me what I've configured

EDWIN: (Reads your user/config.json and summarizes your settings)

Common tasks

I want to... Say this...
See all available skills "List skills"
Switch contexts "Switch to Work" / "Switch to Home" / "Switch to Global"
Create a new skill from my workflow Do the task twice, EDWIN will offer to learn it
Remember a preference "Remember that I prefer X" (see Memory and Wins)
Log an accomplishment "Log a win: shipped the analytics dashboard" (see Memory and Wins)
Get a summary of something "Brief me on [document/article/report]"
Plan a project "Help me plan [project name]"
Brainstorm ideas "Let's brainstorm [topic]" (uses strategist skill)
Learn a concept "Teach me [concept]" (uses tutor skill)
Check my settings "What are my preferences?" or "Show my config"

If something goes wrong

Common issues:

  • EDWIN doesn't recognize a skill trigger: Try being more explicit ("use the briefing skill") or ask EDWIN what skills are available
  • Responses are too verbose or too brief: Adjust your verbosity preference (see above)
  • Can't remember what context I'm in: Ask "what context am I in?"
  • Skills from another context would be useful: Remember, all skills are always available — contexts just prioritize suggestions. You can use any skill in any context.

For more help, see the Troubleshooting guide.
